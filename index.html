A word of welcome

From the Provincial Grand Master

Welcome to the Provincial Grand Lodge of the South Eastern Counties. Within these pages you will find the five lodges meeting in this Province, our forthcoming meetings and events, and a doorway to the further Orders of Irish Freemasonry.

The Provincial Grand Lodge meets four times a year, once each quarter, and every Brother who has received his Master Mason degree is welcome to attend. I encourage the brethren to enjoy the opportunity of visiting other lodges, within this Province and beyond it — for the visitor is always made welcome.

We were to celebrate our 150th anniversary in 2020. The pandemic set that celebration aside, but against the distress of those years it was a small price to bear. If you are reading this with the thought of joining our fraternity, the pages that follow give an honest outline of who we are and what we believe.

About the Craft

An ancient fraternity, plainly explained

Freemasonry teaches morality through the tools and traditions of the medieval builder. Below is a candid account of our origins, our relationship to faith and politics, and the truth about our so-called secrets.

i.

Brotherly Love

Every true Mason regards the whole human species as one family, and extends courtesy and concern to all.

ii.

Relief

To soothe the unhappy and relieve those in distress is the chief business of the fraternity.

iii.

Truth

Truth is the foundation of every virtue; to be good and true is our first lesson.

iv.

Faith

A belief in a Supreme Being is the one unalterable requirement of membership.

Origins +

Freemasonry is many centuries old, and most scholars trace it to the great cathedral-builders of medieval Europe. The skilled travelling craftsmen who raised those structures formed themselves into Guilds — professional societies that maintained the standard of their work and protected the secrets of their trade. In the 17th century, leading citizens who were not stonemasons began to be admitted as honorary, or "speculative", members. As their numbers grew, the lodges came to be composed entirely of such Freemasons. All regular Grand Lodges in the world descend from the three "Mother" Grand Lodges — Ireland, England and Scotland.

Religion & Politics +

Freemasonry is neither anti-religious nor politically orientated. A belief in a Supreme Being is required of every member without exception, and in every lodge the Volume of the Sacred Law lies open. Yet a Mason's relationship with his God is held to be a purely personal matter: the Craft never enquires into it, and no discussion of religion or politics is permitted at a Masonic gathering. Our meetings open and close with prayer that, though sincere, is non-denominational. Freemasonry asks only that its members be peaceable, law-abiding citizens.

Membership & "Secrecy" +

A candidate is admitted by degrees, each a colourful ceremony handed down from the distant past, and each accompanied by a solemn promise. What is genuinely secret is only the means by which one Mason may prove himself to another — a survival from the days when membership cards did not exist and men relied on word and sign. Freemasonry is therefore a society with secrets, not a "secret society": its constitution, rules, meeting places and members are well known, and its headquarters in Molesworth Street, Dublin, stand within a hundred yards of the seat of government.

What & Who +

Freemasons are ordinary people who try to live by high moral principles and to be good citizens. The Craft teaches kindness, honesty, decency, courtesy, fairness and concern, and holds above all that we are part of the Brotherhood of Man under the Fatherhood of God. Its members are drawn from every walk of life and every social station — men, in other words, perhaps much like yourself.

The path of the Mason

The Orders of Irish Freemasonry

In Ireland the Craft is the foundation. From it a Brother may progress, by degrees, through five further Orders — each self-governing, each with its own colour and symbol, all in harmony with the Grand Lodge.

Blue
The foundation

The Craft — Blue Masonry

Governed by the Grand Lodge of Ireland

The three symbolic degrees worked in every lodge, and the doorway to all that follows. Every Freemason begins here, and no further Order may be entered without it. The square and compasses is its emblem; blue, its signature colour.

Red
The completion of the Craft

Mark & Royal Arch

Governed by the Supreme Grand Royal Arch Chapter of Ireland

Regarded as the completion of pure ancient Masonry. A candidate is first advanced as a Mark Master Mason, then exalted to the Holy Royal Arch — a Chapter uniquely and directly recognised by the Grand Lodge of Ireland. Chapters are led not by a single Master but by three Principals, and members are styled Companions. The triple tau is its emblem; red, its colour.

Green
An Order peculiar to Ireland

Knight Masonry

Governed by the Grand Council of Knight Masons of Ireland

Three remarkably elaborate degrees, distinctly Old Testament in character and built upon the legend of Zerubbabel and the return from Babylon. Long conferred in various bodies, they were normalised in 1923 under a Grand Council, and the Irish working is held to be the most complete in the world. Its members are Knights; green, its colour.

The philosophic degrees

Ancient & Accepted Rite

Governed by the Supreme Council for Ireland

A progressive series of degrees from the eighteenth to the thirty-third, of which the Rose Croix (18°) is the best known. Christian in character and entered by invitation, it develops the moral and philosophical lessons of the Craft. The double-headed eagle is its emblem.

The chivalric Orders

Knights Templar & Malta

Governed by the Great Priory of Ireland

The Christian chivalric Orders of the Temple and of Malta, worked in Preceptories. Admission is by invitation only, open to those who profess the Christian faith and a belief in the Holy Trinity. In Ireland a candidate must be a subscribing Master Mason of at least five years' standing and a Royal Arch Companion of at least two. The cross patée is its emblem — In Hoc Signo Vinces.

    Charities & festivals

    Lodges of the Province support both the Grand Lodge charities and a local provincial fund, raising money quietly throughout the year. Grand Lodge works with the Charity Regulator in Dublin and the Charity Commission of Northern Ireland to keep every Masonic charity fully compliant.

    The Milne Watt Boyce Fund

    Made up of donations from the brethren of the Province, this fund assists Brethren or their dependents in distress and provides Christmas gifts to our annuitants. Cases of need are brought to the Provincial Grand Almoner by the Lodge Almoner, once the lodge has first granted aid from its own funds.

    Information on the national charities and special fund-raising sits with Grand Lodge. See The Grand Lodge Charities and The Grand Master's Festival.

    A distinguished company

    Notable Irish Freemasons

    Across three centuries, the Craft in Ireland has counted soldiers, statesmen, writers and explorers among its brethren — and one remarkable lady.

    EA

    The Hon. Elizabeth Aldworth

    1695 – 1775
    Lodge No. 44, Doneraile Court, 1712

    Born Elizabeth St Leger, daughter of the 1st Viscount Doneraile of Co. Cork. Having watched a lodge held in her father's house through a gap in the wall, she was discovered — and the brethren resolved to initiate her. "The Lady Freemason" remains the only woman ever admitted to regular Freemasonry, and became a lifelong patroness of the Craft.

    R

    Richard Parsons, Earl of Rosse

    c. 1682 – 1741
    First Grand Master of Ireland, 1725

    Installed as the first recorded Grand Master of the Grand Lodge of Ireland in June 1725 — the moment organised Freemasonry in Ireland steps into the historical record. His election marks the beginning of the second-oldest Grand Lodge in the world.

    W

    The Duke of Wellington

    1769 – 1852
    Lodge No. 494, Trim, Co. Meath, 1790

    Arthur Wellesley, born in Dublin, was initiated in his family's lodge at Trim under the Irish Constitution. Victor of Waterloo and twice Prime Minister of Britain, he stands among the most consequential figures ever raised in an Irish lodge.

    O'C

    Daniel O'Connell

    1775 – 1847
    Lodge No. 189, Dublin, 1799

    "The Liberator", who won Catholic Emancipation for Ireland. Initiated in Dublin and briefly Master of his lodge, he later withdrew from the Craft following the Church's condemnation — yet the fraternity long toasted his memory.

    OW

    Oscar Wilde

    1854 – 1900
    Apollo University Lodge No. 357, Oxford, 1875

    The Dublin-born poet and playwright was initiated while a student at Oxford and took the Mark degree soon after. He is said to have relished the ceremony and its regalia as much as any of his celebrated wit.

    S

    Sir Ernest Shackleton

    1874 – 1922
    Navy Lodge No. 2612, London, 1901

    Born at Kilkea, Co. Kildare, the great Antarctic explorer was made a Mason on the eve of his years of polar exploration — endurance, leadership and loyalty being virtues the Craft and the ice alike demand.
